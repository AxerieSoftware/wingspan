Monarch's Cash Flow shows where your money went. Projected balances shows what's coming: checking day by day ahead, with the paychecks, bills, card payments and everyday spending you can expect. You see a low point before you hit it.

It sits on **Cash Flow**, under Monarch's chart. At the top of Cash Flow, **Monthly** looks as far ahead as free cash is kept safe (30, 60 or 90 days, or the next payday if that's later), **Quarterly** 90 days and **Yearly** a year.

<Screenshot name="projected-balances" clip="projected-balances" alt="Projected balances on Cash Flow: checking day by day ahead, free cash today and the monthly surplus." />

## Free cash today

How much you could move out of checking today to spend, save or pay a card down early. Every bill still gets paid, every card's minimum is met, and the amount you keep in checking stays untouched for the weeks ahead and through the next payday.

If you budget toward goals in Monarch, free cash splits into **to goals**, already promised to them, and **truly free**. It also appears in Recurring's month summary, next to your bills.

<Screenshot name="free-cash" alt="Recurring's month summary with the Statements line and Free cash today, linking to Projected balances." />

## Reading the card

- **Free cash today**: what's safe to move out of checking, with the date it's safe through and the amount it keeps in checking.
- **Short from Oct 14**, in red, replaces it when checking would drop below what you keep, showing the gap at its lowest. There's no free cash until that's covered.
- **Monthly surplus**: what an average month leaves after income, bills and everyday spending. Spending on your cards counts, not the payments to them, and a card outside Monarch counts its typical payment. Below $0, you fall behind a little each month.
- **Credit left**: the lowest your available credit gets on the cards you borrow on, and when one would max out.
- **Checking runs out**: the first day checking would go below $0 even after borrowing on your cards and moving money from backup accounts, or **Stays above $0** if it doesn't.
- **+$1,782.64 net by Nov 10**, next to the title: how much checking grows or shrinks over the range, with nothing borrowed. It's red only when checking ends below what you keep.

### The chart

- The solid line is **Checking**, red where it's below what you keep. The dashed line, **If cards were paid off**, is checking minus what the cards owe.
- The dot marks the lowest checking gets in the range, like **Low $1,850 · Oct 24**.
- The green band is free cash, drawn through the date it's safe for.
- Green marks are paydays, orange marks are card payments, and red or yellow marks flag a problem, like a card maxing out.
- Hover a day for its balance and what came in or went out.

## Setting it up

On **Cash Flow**, open **Edit cash and cards**, or use the pencil next to Free cash today on Recurring.

<Screenshot name="cash-and-cards" alt="The Cash and cards dialog, choosing checking accounts, reserves, cards and how much to keep in checking." />

- **Cash accounts**: mark each as **Checking**, where bills and card payments come from, **Backup** you'd move money from if checking ran low, or **Not counted**.
- **Always keep**: a cushion that stays in checking. Free cash never counts it.
- **Keep free cash safe for**: 30, 60 or 90 days ahead, or until the next paycheck if that's later.
- **Cards to borrow on if checking runs low**: every card's balance, statements and spending are always counted. The plan borrows on the cards that are on, from the top of the list, then moves money from backup accounts. Turn a card off to never borrow on it.

Until you save, Wingspan guesses: accounts named "checking" are checking, your other cash accounts are backup, and every card can be borrowed on, lowest APR first. A card added to Monarch later can be borrowed on until you turn it off.

A warning next to a card means Monarch has no credit limit for it, so the plan can't borrow on it. Add the limit in the card's details in Monarch.

If you use Monarch's businesses, the household and each business have their own settings. Pick one under **Settings for**.

## What it counts

- Monarch's recurring income and bills paid from or into your checking or your cards, on the due days you've set. One with no account is counted in checking.
- Wingspan's [bills](/features/manual-bills/) and [card payments](/features/card-payments/)
- [Expected income](/features/expected-income/), at what you usually take in
- Everyday spending, at your usual pace from recent months, including money you move from checking to accounts it doesn't follow, like savings or a brokerage
- Each card's statement, paid as far as checking allows, with anything left over carrying interest
- Bills that are already late

Pending purchases count as already spent, and pending deposits count once they clear, so free cash never runs ahead of what's there.

## Good to know

- A card with no due date is assumed due in 30 days. Add a [card payment](/features/card-payments/) to set it.
- If Monarch also tracks one of your Wingspan bills or incomes as recurring, it counts once, from Monarch's.
- In Monarch, categorize credit card payments and moves between your own accounts as **Transfer**. Otherwise a card payment counts as spending on top of the card's own spending.
- **Couldn't refresh from Monarch** means you're seeing the last numbers that loaded. Choose **Try again**.

:::note
Monarch Plus forecasts years ahead. Projected balances looks up to a year ahead, day by day. If Monarch's forecasting adds that, Wingspan will remove Projected balances.
:::
