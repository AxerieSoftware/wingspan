Monarch counts loan payments as spending. A month you put an extra $1,000 toward a student loan looks the same as a month you spent $1,000, and your savings rate drops even though you're better off.

Loans paid down shows the part of those payments that lowered what you owe. It sits after Monarch's **Savings rate**, with the amount and, in parentheses, its share of your income.

<Screenshot name="loans-paid-down" alt="Cash Flow's totals: Income, Expenses, Total savings and Savings rate, then Loans paid down at $340 (78%) and Interest a month at $49." />

## Where it shows

- **Cash Flow**, for the month, quarter or year you pick.
- **Reports**, on the **Cash flow** tab, for the dates you choose. The share uses the total income Reports shows, with your filters applied. While a report covers all time there's no start date to measure from, so the card waits until you pick dates.

Hover **Loans paid down** for the dates it covers and how much each loan went down.

## How it's counted

- It's how much your loans' balances went down over the period, through today if it's still going: what you paid minus the interest added. It's negative when interest added more.
- A loan paid off and closed during the period counts down to $0.
- A loan added during the period isn't counted, so new debt doesn't show up as a negative paydown.
- With Monarch's businesses, it counts the loans of whatever the page shows: Household, a business, or both.

## Good to know

- Balances come from Monarch, so a loan you track by hand counts once you update its balance. [Update balance](/features/interest-cost/#keeping-a-manual-loan-current) estimates it for you.
- The card is hidden when no loan shown had a balance at the start of the period.
- **Unavailable** means Monarch's loan balances didn't load. Wingspan tries again a minute later.
